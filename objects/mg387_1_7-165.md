They had discussion for a while, promised to deliver some sort of official response after consulting with the member of each barrack and ended the meeting. 

We were asked for our opinion on this by Mr. Sakimura. Mr. Takeoka, Mr. Usuda and Mr. Horagami joined us to discuss various items. Mr. Horagami's opinion is that it is not wise to go against the military at this point as it is unpredictable how many more years we will be forced to live under the current circumstance, and we should expect more demands and requirements issued in the future. The majority of his Unit 7 members agreed to work outside of the fence to a certain extent. However, something like prohibiting sending telegrams is not acceptable, and he offered a detailed explanation about how we will emphasize our cooperation with them instead of responding to the administration's threats to stop or eliminate services at their mercy. Mr. Usuda was insisting that we should be more aggressive and had some small arguments with Mr. Sakimura, who seems to be with the military side. After all, we decided to peacefully accept their request this time and to make sure they understand we are elderly and could not fulfill some tasks in the heat depending on what is required. We all agreed at the end that it is appropriate to offer our labor as long as it is too hard for us, and that we will send our response to them.  

Treats made by this officer to close some facilities is unjust and inhumane. It is clearly against the Geneva Conventions. I assume they will take this authoritarian position every time something comes up in the future.
