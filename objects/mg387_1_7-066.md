We assume the headquarters has a legal department and requested more information from the department, and it was reported that they agreed on it. We were told that the deadline to provide a yes or not answer to the questionnaire has been extended from the original deadline at noon tomorrow due to the situation, and the officers are to hold responsibility for the extension. 

While we were in Missoula, we were told there is some information about exchange possibilities with diplomats and international merchants between the two countries and those who desire to go back could be added to the list . I have made up my mind right after the order to be moved from Missoula to be added to the list after careful consideration. I will of course answer yes no matter what. 

Next on agenda is the last item, and it was reported that the military headquarters administration announced that the Japanese internees at the camp are to be treated as war prisoners and that they issued an order that the above mentioned questionnaire is due to be submitted by 2 p.m. on the 18th. I answered yes right away,  added a note to say as soon as possible and submitted it. Nishino, Fukuda, Sato, Kobayashi (Astoria), Nakamaura, Ishibashi, Shinome, Morinaga, Kato (Akira), Matsuura are the ones who answered yes among the fellow citizens from Oregon and Wapato. There are a total of about 520 or 530 of us including almost all from Panama and Hawaii, and approximately half from California. 

It is unbearably noisy in the camp now with this yes or no question as each of us is selfishly trying to gain higher ground and insisting on his own opinion on this issue.
