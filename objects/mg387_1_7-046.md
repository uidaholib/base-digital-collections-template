216

It looked like those who are in poor health like Mr. Tsuboi and disabled people needing crutches had already had dinner, and they were transported in a car as the first group. 
20 to 30 soldiers were lined up keeping a certain distance from each other on both sides of us, guarding their rifles and watching us. Upon "Load up your firearm" commanded by the commanding officer , they loaded up their guns with rattling sounds and held them at the ready. Everything was so frightful. However, we all have experienced such treatment to us civilians when we left Po [1] City. That did not frighten us any longer. It was just like watching an army drill, and that is because we absolutely do not have anything to be ashamed of as a resident of the U.S. 

We marched into the mess hall. Soldiers were lined up on both sides. I had this faint worrisome idea thinking it would be horrible if even one of them tripped over something and his gun discharges a bullet, but I am sure the safety locks were on. When we reached in front of the facility for the Italians, we heard such a loud cheer from them saying "Hurray Japan, Japan". Shouting from nearly one thousand Italian sailors sounded powerful enough to have the Rocky Mountains crumble, and banzai calls in response from us arose one after another. 

Mess hall was overcrowded. Boxed meal with two rice balls, omelet and pickles was for our night snack on the train. We marched to the train about one mile away after a meal guarded by soldiers, and I was carrying my drawstring bag and coat. A long walk was highly painful for the elders and us who have been detained for months. 


[1] Portland?
