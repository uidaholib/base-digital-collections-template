Printed questionnaires about going back to the home county will be distributed by the camp headquarters, and according to this, they are to find out if we would be interested in voluntarily going back to Japan if there is possibility to be repatriated. We are to required to fill out our U.S. address and the Japanese address, as well as names of dependents, our wives and children, who we will take back with us. 

Therefore, this is an extremely important matter, and leaders from each tent should hold a meeting to discuss it. We should avoid signing  such documents without paying attention to details, and we came to the conclusion that it would be most kind done by the leaders to directly meet with the authorities to understand the intention behind it, and we asked  officers from the headquarters to attend a meeting with us and asked our questions. The most important matter we need to ask about is the meaning of repatriation. We asked if it would be impossible to come back to the U.S. when peacetime comes if we voluntarily give up our permanent residence status that we have been granted, and the answer given by the officers is "That is our understanding". However, they also said that is just their personal interpretation, and they cannot offer legal interpretations for sure. They expressed their sympathy toward us and said they understand we would hesitate answering yes.
