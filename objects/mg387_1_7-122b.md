June 30 (Tuesday)

I sent a reply to Mr. Kimimoto. I feel so moved with the young person's friendship toward me. 

174 of fellow Japanese arrived at four in the afternoon after being held for several days for various reasons. They are all sent by guards from Tennessee, but almost everyone except for a few is prominent Japanese from Honolulu in the mainland Hawaii. They are the first victims after the war broke out, sent to the Wisconsin State from San Francisco and relocated to the Tennessee State one month after that. They ended up being detained here after spending one month in the camp. 

Among them is lieutenant Nakamaki, a submarine soldier who was involved in the Honolulu attack. He was assigned to a different living unit outside our barracks due to his officer status. Shortly after all of us got settled down, we saw the officer guarded by one guard (with a baton but not armed) and a fellow Japanese carrying a blue bag (with the officer's belongings in it) escorted to outside of the fence through scorching an inside pass. He was wearing a supplied cap and clothing and looking well-groomed in spite of the heat we are having here.
