It is fortunate that I am finally getting used to it and no longer feeling dizzy. May, June and July is the three-month rainy season here, and rain falling with thunder lightning offers a sort of coolness. There is a leak in the barrack roof every time it rains, and we are forced to move our beds around. The assigned officer came to check the leak once, promised a repair and left, but the promise was not executed no matter how many times it rained after that. This is another aspect of a war.

Poisonous Snakes

We have poisonous snakes in the facility, and we have already caught some of them. They are powerful enough that once you get bit, the poison travels all around in  your body and kills you within several hours. One fellow caught an about 4-foot beautifully colored snake. One soldier saw it and told us that it's an important sort of snake that kills poisonous ones. He told us not to kill this kind as they are helpful to get rid of poisonous snakes. Nature is alway good at controlling itself. 

Baby Turtles

The ground in the facility is like clay does not absorb water. There is a lot of sand at the same time. The humidity is high despite the extremely high temperature. Maybe that is the reason why baby turtles come up once in a while. Some people catch them and keep them. Fireflies are not common, but we sometimes see them. Poisonous snakes, baby turtles and fireflies. What a strange contrast.
