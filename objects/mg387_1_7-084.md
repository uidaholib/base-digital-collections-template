The above report was presented in two segments, from 11:40 am to 1 pm and also from 2:25 pm to 3 pm on May 4th at a separate room in the camp. I recognized it was extremely efficiently conducted by the presenting representatives. I believe that this will help in improving the living conditions for us. I hope they work on creating a harmonious environment among the fellow internees and do their best. 

Reported as above. Mr. Young from the Department of State expressed his opinion about treatment of U.S. citizens living in Japan and mentioned that they are treated well in Japan, but the condition is very bad in Hong Kong, Manila and other locations. He said it is the way things go during a wartime and seemed to support the policy to forcefully separate our families. However, the response from the Consul General of Spain was direct and clear, and also sincere, and his comments impressed the Japanese representatives.

Mr. Takeoka went on and presented his report on the relocation in the afternoon as follows.
It is determined that we will be relocated again to somewhere within the next month. It is unclear if we are to receive a notice one day prior to the departure or a last-minute notice three hours in advance. We should be prepared to act quickly by packing our belongings and having only items used daily out for us to use. The destination has not been announced, but it has been announced that it will be somewhere in a higher altitude than where we currently are, at a location approximately 2,000 feet above sea level. It was also reported that there will be no tax on the postage starting tomorrow, but air mails and special mails require stamps.
