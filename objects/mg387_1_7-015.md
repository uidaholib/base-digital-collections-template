205

According to the letter to Mr. Yasui from his son, who graduated from the University of Michigan, although he is a U. S. citizen, graduated from the University with high academic achievement and has been offered a guaranteed job position, was denied employment simply because he is a child of a Japanese. He is one of the most qualified to be a loyal citizen to the nation and serve behind guns. He expressed his frustration over the abandonment by the very nation he pledges his legion due to racial prejudice. Moreover, his long-term friends did not do a thing to help when his father was detained. What is friendship for? He is angry and thinks the friends must have been holding some different feelings toward his father even when they were socializing with the father. It is ironic and pitiful to see that many nisei who have been educated as a U.S. citizen, and grown up in a family with the parents who are still attached to the homeland, are suffering from large dogmatic issues when a war breaks out between Japan and the U.S.    
One person has been brought over and detained from Yakima this afternoon. 

According to a report broadcasted on a radio news (Mr. Nakajo reported to us), a so-called "Reception Center" in Owens Valley is currently under construction where issei and nisei residing in the Los Angeles area in California are schedule to be sent to due to the removal order. Barracks to hold 250 people each are being built, and a total of forty-eight barracks are scheduled to be built in the entire area. (Male workers will be paid 30 dollars a month for a four-hours-per-day labor schedule in compliance with the Civilian Conservation Corps. Those who are interested can apply according to another radio news)
