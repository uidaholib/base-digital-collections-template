However, he lost his opportunity to die in action caused by uncontrollable fate, and what he feels inside as a brave soldier reminds us that we also cannot take it easy. I heard the Lieutenant has his mouth shut and never answers any questions from his fellow Japanese. He probably decided not to say anything until he reports to his supervisor after he goes back to Japan, and it was probably the order by the U.S. Military as well. I heard that a fellow exchange passenger asked where he is from and promised not to tell anyone before they departed while detained in Tennessee. The officer thanked him and told the passenger that he was not able to answer the question for some reason. I am even having a hard time sleeping at night having his image in my head. We can maintain our lives here thanks to brave soldiers like him. I wish we could get to know of his brave actions, share them among fellow Japanese and offer our appreciation and consolation. 

There was announcement delivered by the administration that they will establish the Recreation Department and create sections such as Library, Movies, Gardening, Education and others. Another announcement delivered at the same time is that they will set up walking time outside of the fence twice a day, three times a week on Mondays, Thursdays and Fridays. They told us to get together at the gate in advance if we would like to take a walk and that it would be a one-hour walk. It will be a lot better to freely walk around within the fence than taking a walk led by a guard. Those who are silly enough are welcome to participate.
