205

A big cross was constructed by the fence in the open field in front of the 24th barrack by christians in the camp several days ago. Easter prayer service was held at 7 a.m. today led by Mr. Ban with prayer by Reverend Nakajo and Reverend Matsunaga. There is a vast wild field with a range of mountains far away in the background and just a few lonely small trees spotting near and far. Dawn has passed, and the rising sun cast shadows on the mountain rims. It is so refreshing to hear robins chirping somewhere. Fresh air in early spring morning waves into dreadful barbed wire fences. I saw three fellow detainees already praying, quietly and motionlessly, in front of the cross. There were lights on here and there in rows of barracks, probably because people woke up early to attend the Easter service today. Shortly after, attendees dressed in distributed clothing came out from each barrack and gathered around the cross through different passes. Everyone looked sad. No one displayed joyful positiveness toward life with faith and love of Christ. It looked like a crowd of gray-haired and bald-headed outcasts we were becoming to be, struck with frustration, sorrow or hopelessness. It was a truly sad Easter celebration. I saw not only the Christians, but many people who have identified themselves as Buddhist and elders who have been voluntarily working for the Buddhist Association to promote the religion were also  in front of the cross, listening to the prayers with their heads down and asking for loving protection by Christ with hymns sung around them.
