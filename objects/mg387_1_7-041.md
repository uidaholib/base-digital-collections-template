211

Mr. Akio Matsushita made a small drawstring bag out of pieces from old overalls to carry personal items for the train ride. It is not a good-looking one but sure is perfect memorabilia. 

Small Buddha birth service was held in the 19th barracks in the afternoon. 

Arrays of Japanese food were served for dinner prepared with items gifted by Ambassador Nomura. Rice cakes sent by the ambassador through the Consul General of Spain were also distributed to each barrack. Each of us has been busy sending telegrams to the family to let them know we are being transferred. 

It seems that no one could sleep at all the night before last as people got up to go to the latrine one after another. It was a bit calmer last night, but the place looks empty after all the items are gone, and everyone carries sadness on their face. Letters to those who are relocating are being prioritized by the inspectors and processed with unusual efforts to deliver to the people earlier than the other. 

People from the other barracks come for signatures on their memory books one after another, and it is quite bothersome. 

I heard Mr. Ito was suddenly exempted from the transfer, designated to remain and ordered to work at the laundry facility. It did not happen to the other people working at the laundry facility, and everyone finds it odd that he is the only one who was ordered not to transfer. Even Mr. Muto (Los Angeles) who had been a chef to feed about 1,000 people until today is listed to be transferred.
