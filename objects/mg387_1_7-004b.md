March 25 (Wednesday)

It will be a full three months since we all were incarcerated in this camp in three days. While a lot of people here were living without any purpose, the world outside the camp is constantly changing, with bright achievements made in the East filled with hopes for permanent peace established in the region. According to the newspaper, Japan occupied a gold mine in New Guinea, and it is reported that steady mining is continuing under its control and expected to yield 60,000,000 yen annually. However, hardship is casting upon the lives of its people in the nations on the U.S. and British sector, and a dark future is forecasted for them. 

I'm not sure about my fellow residents of the 25th barracks. They are gaining a certain level of calmness and endurance, but at the same time, frustration and the feeling of missing life is boiling up at the bottom of our hearts. Those feelings surface and burst, and it is such a pity to have them explode on occasions day after day. I cannot help feeling sorry for everyone.

I do not find it a pointless endeavor to write up a profile of fellows in the 25th barrack mainly from Po [1] City to record and celebrate their lives.  

I could see that everyone finally learned to maintain the calmness, but we are still extremely restless, 

[1] Portland City?
