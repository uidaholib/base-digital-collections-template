443

It is the right thing to do for the U.S. government to consider the old age of the internees while promoting their civil right protector image. We should be allowed to raise our voice against the high temperatures here. 

I have not had anything but milk after breakfast for the last several days. I saw articles by American reporters who recently arrived at Lurenzo Margnes in newspapers. They are all generally bad mouthing about the treatment of American internees in Japan. What caught our attention among those articles is that 600 people were killed or injured by air raids over Tokyo by the U.S. Navy. Prime Minister Tojo immediately offered his apology to the imperial office about the enemy airplanes flying over the imperial castle, and two high ranking officers committed suicide. One threw a hammer at the prime minister and injured him. Tokyo is utterly defenseless against the enemy air raids, and the government is begging people to be engaged in fire fighting instead of seeking underground shelters. Second lieutenant Sakamaki does not own a watch or have a guard, and he is very late for meals as he is alway guessing what time they are served. That causes a lot of operational problems for the mess hall workers. We were told to accept the situation as it is an inconvenience for us as well as the second lieutenant. Our barrack members made a suggestion to purchase a dollar watch  and give it to him. Fortunately Mr. Shinonome had a spare pocket watch, and we immediately offered it to the second lieutenant. He accepted it.
