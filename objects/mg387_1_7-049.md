219

April 11 (Saturday)

Train running through a dark night does not give us any idea where we were. 

We arrived at a fairly big town at 6 a.m. The sign says Billings. It is summer saving time and would have been at 5 a.m. It was a pretty lonely place with a few lights in sight. Ours was a special train, and of course no one was boarding or deboarding. 

The train just kept running in the middle of a wild and vast field, and no matter where I looked, it was just an endless empty field in faded colors as far as I could see. I am glad regular meals suddenly started to be served. Paper plates on tin trays. Paper spoons and knives are still used just like they were on the way from Po [1] city to Missoula. 

After lunch, a seemingly non-commissioned officer was walking through telling us that we were going through a scenic area called Winged River Canyon. There are tall and rocky mountains on both sides. I also saw a small stream running in the outskirts, but the water was muddy. During the trip through the continent, there was a sudden big change of scenery. 

We arrived at a town called Casper at 4:15 p.m. While the train was there, men and women were looking at us Japanese with a strange look in their eyes. Guard soldiers got off the train every time we stopped and lined up on both sides of the train guarding. They chased people away if they tried to come closer to the train.


[1] Portland?
