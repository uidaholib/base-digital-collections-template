May 4 (Monday)

Buddhism memorial service for late Mr. Otomo was held in the hallway in a camp facility. All fellow Japanese lined up on both sides of the front part of the east gate to receive the coffin when it was time and put it down on the most shabby looking altar set up at the west part of the space. We all lined up in a column and gave him our most respectful solute. Initially, Catholic service was scheduled to be conducted by the army, but they found out the deceased was Buddhist and quickly changed it to Buddhist service. 54 Buddhist monks from Hawaii and three from the mainland chanted the sutras. Such service is rare to see these days. I was located toward the end of the line and just managed to see the back of a section of the monks, but the area around the coffin was occupied by people from Panama. I also saw Consul General of Spain and a clerk from the Department of State, who happened to be visiting at time, were joining in the line with the camp officers behind the altar.
