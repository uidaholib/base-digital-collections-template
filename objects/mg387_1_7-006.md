116

Another figure as angry and bad-mouthed as Mr. Ito is Mr. Yasui. This person could never have had a hearing while he was in a county jail if I had not said anything. I imagine he should have been prepared to be sent directly to a camp. He was detained due to the fact that he lived nearby a military shipyard regardless of his relationship to the management of Japanese associations. Therefore, it is definitely inevitable [to be sent to a camp] for those who have been continuously serving in leadership positions in our fellow community. The fact of war is that all the fellow citizens in power are to be sent to a concentration camp. Mr. Yasui put one elbow on the bed and shed his tears when I said that to him. I did not have a word to continue our conversation when I saw how sadly hurt and exhausted he was in front of me. I just left his unit and told other fellows how he was. 

Mr. Yasui was constantly reading the Bible, singing hymns and praying and was respected by us all for his solemn attitude. However, his recent remarks are hard to hear without our faces turned away from him. He is aware of the war, but he has lost the calmness of his mind to quietly observe the changes of social situation and national governance caused by the war. On top of that, his cursing against the U.S. is loud and vulgar and is unbearable to hear. Worst of all is his horrible voice when he loudly speaks with a thick accent with the speed of a machine gun no matter where he is as if he is forcing others to agree or to add. His loud and sharp tongue in the bathroom is receiving disgust from people around him.
