355

I don't know where we will be sent out this time, but it is very bothersome to be hauled around like a herd of sheep. I have to say it would have been the better option to move us to the location directly from Missoula. 

I have read The Oregonian thoroughly today, after being unable to do so for a while. The articles are ordinary under the news censorship. 

A gift of various groceries  from a group of about 40 housewives living in Po [2] city was delivered when we departed from Missoula, and Dr. Tanaka paid for the shipment and asked them to make a special delivery to this location. It was finally brought to us today and distributed to each tent. Each tent received several servings of fukujinzuke pickles, funyu [3], jarred stewed nori seaweed and others. 

It has been cloudy and muggy all day today. We heard thunder in the evening. 

We heard a gunshot through the silence of the night from the east side of the fence last night. I could hear people coming out of the tent and making noises here and there, trying to inspect if something unusual happened to our fellow Japanese. I heard it in my half dreamy state and did not think it was worth fussing about. I was told this morning that a soldier accidentally fired his gun by a warehouse somewhere. 

It is Boys' Festival Day today. The typical treat kashiwamochi is nowhere to be seen.
