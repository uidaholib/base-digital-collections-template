371

I got to have the end seats all for myself. The train started to move shortly after, ran slowly in the facility, stopped and departed blowing a whistle. It was 10:30 am.

We traveled through Lawton. It was reported that the city is recently suffering from a housing shortage due to increased population caused by military industries development and others. A quick look from a train window did not reveal anything though. 

We are traveling through the south end part of the continent by train, and all we can see is a vast wild field or large farms as expected. It is characteristic of the South to see black people by the railroad. Interestingly, you can tell if black or white people living in the farm houses peppering the field by looking at laundry drying out. It is certain that black people live in the house if you see clothes in bright colors. Unlike the trip across Wyoming, I see trees, and green farm fields as far as we can see display the richness of the region. It is still the monotonous scenery of the continent though. I see cactus flowers growing in weird shapes by the railroad, and it is a rare sight for us who alway lived in an urban area. 

We crossed the Oklahoma State border and kept running in the part of Texas. Scenery outside the windows was nothing but boring for our eyes. We spent more than one and a half hours at the Dallas station, the only city in Texas. It is getting to dawn, and neon signs are turned on. Where is the world war taking place? People are looking at our train as it is unusual but do not have any nervous expression on their face. Vastness of the U.S. just hit me.
