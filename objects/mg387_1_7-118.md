388

She wrote she hopes I will go back to Japan to rest and come back again when peaceful time comes back. I am so appreciative of her kindness as usual and cannot help shedding tears.

Mr. Nakatani sent me 15 dollars under the name of  Mrs. Kiso. He said that is a part of the money the newspaper publisher collected, and he is still holding a bit more amount, but it takes enormous work to cash the assets. He wrote that he is going to send me a check sometime soon, but I hope he will not as it will be nothing but a piece of paper in this camp. 

Ms. Nobuko seems to misunderstand what I wrote to her and sent me her apologies for not able to negotiate with Mr. Johnson as she is not able to go out. 

I heard all Japanese books will be confiscated when we leave for Japan. I decided to write down all the waka and haiku poems I have written since I was sent to Missoula in a separate notebook. 

Mountain top ranges lit red in the dark blue sky, I am feeling sad in Missoula (January 9, 1942)

Care package full of daily items and kind thoughts from Mrs. Kiso was delivered (January 2)

A few moments while unpacking the package, I could not hold back my tears and sobbing. (evening on January 2)

Big icicles are hanging from each barrack roof like swords, and it is getting dark in the cold (January 10)
