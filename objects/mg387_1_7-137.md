407 

What I am most surprised about is that he received a letter from his only daughter while detained in Fort Sill, and she said in the letter, "we need to defeat Jap [11] in this war. I don't like Japanese people and would like to leave them and move somewhere in Oklahoma to be closer to my father" and so on. I can tell what kind of education he offers to his family, and at the same time, I think we need to reevaluate Mr. Sakimura to determine if he is a trustworthy Japanese. 

Mr. Zenichi Ono, from Okayama Prefecture, agriculture

He also has been my barrack mate since we stayed in a tent in Fort Sill. He has a strong sense of responsibility and is also a pious Christian. I have already described his character in the previous page and will skip details, but he has some health issues these days and is not able to move around as he would like to. He feels as if that was his own fault and very sorry for that. However, there is a saying that goes: a family in good behavior will be blessed. He does not need to worry about anything. We are all wishing him regaining his health from the bottom of our heart. We would like him to take care of himself and hope plenty of rest will bring back his health although we do not know how long this living situation will last. 

Mr. Shigeo [Nobuo [12]] Okazaki, from Okayama Prefecture, Konkokyo priest

I have also written about him while I was in Missoula. He has been having some serious health issues ever since he moved to Livingston. I heard something is wrong with his kidneys on top of his high blood pressure.


[11] This is the word used in the journal

[12] At least two possible readings for the first name
