368

My friends from Northwest have mixed feelings. However, we all are dissatisfied with living in a leaky tent with dust and wind, and everyone seems somewhat hopeful assuming that we are not moving to somewhere worse than this place. However, we will all miss our lives here as well. Home is where you make it. Some people say they got used to this place, and if not living in a tent, they would not like to move away. It is human nature and also the honest feelings of people who have been living overseas far away from the homeland for decades. 

Death of another fellow Japanese
It was announced that Mr. Itsuharu [1] Shimoda from California, who was forced to be hospitalized on the 12th, finally passed away. It sounded like he was making a gradual recovery but developed a violent personality, and unfortunately died as a result of injection by a military doctor. It ｗas reported that the cause of death was encephalitis triggered by syphilis. 

A Buddhist memorial service was held as always in the morning on the 26th. Roll call has been conducted since that day with everyone gathered on the pass on the south gate of the wire fence. It is extremely painful to keep standing in around 100 degrees heat in the afternoon for elders and especially for people with health issues. This continued until our departure. 

The flowers Mr. Ono planted in front of the seventh tent are blooming and attracting attention from a lot of people.
