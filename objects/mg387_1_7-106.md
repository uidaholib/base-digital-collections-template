He also made a statement that we were orderly and cooperative in Camp Sill, and that he is expecting the same attitude constantly displayed in this camp as well. His remarks were received with applause, and he was sent off with us cheerfully saying "goodbye" to him. It was an unexpected exchange of friendship between the military and unarmed Japanese civilians living in the U.S. during the wartime. 

Meals

Meals in this camp are poor, and they are no comparison to what was served in Camp Sill. The portion is also so small that it is creating a lot of complaints. There is of course no way that they are offering a special diet for someone like me who is suffering from digestive system problems. First of all, bowls and plates are for soldiers, and they are beyond my description. They have a certain type of smell, and it does not stimulate my appetite. 

I am surprised to see brave folks who are ready to devour even a plate while complaining about the quality of the food. There are two types of complaints about food, quantity and quality, and when the former type of people say "the food is bad", it means "the portion is too small". I would like to advocate higher quality food. I understand they have a limited amount of food, but there should be some room for improvement on nutrients and taste. I am afraid that some low ranked soldiers are skimming off our food or they are not working hard to serve Jap [3]. In that case, there should be something we can do to improve the situation.

Wooden Clogs

Someone made a pair of geta wooden clogs and started to wear them. Everyone is good with their hands. Mr. Sakimura made a pair for me, and Mr. Ono is making traditional straps for me.
