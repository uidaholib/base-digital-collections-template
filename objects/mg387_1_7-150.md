420

- As for washing and pressing our clothes, we currently  do not know how many more years we are to live in this facility, but we are not provided with any services for moth control reasons. The response is that the administration will arrange services provided by local vendors in the nearby cities for those who are scheduled to go back to Japan, but the cost should be covered by the customers. We requested the same arrangements to be made for the other internees as it is needed, but we were told that it is not possible without the approval of the headquarters.They offered a compromise that they will have no objections for us to use the name of the people who are going back to Japan to access such services. 

- As for free access between the J area and the K area, this is where fellow Japanese from Panama and Hawaii are interned, and a half of the newcomers from Hawaii this time are also interned in barracks in the same area. However, free access between the both areas is prohibited, and it is extremely meaningless and unreasonable to obtain permission from a guard every time we visit the other area back and forth. 

- Colonel Dunn seemed puzzled and told us that he believed the visiting back and forth freely between the areas was permitted, and it definitely should be allowed as we wish. After hearing about the current situation, he concluded that we should get approval from the supervising officer. I don't know if the orders from the top management have not been delivered, or if it is taking the guards some time to execute the orders although they know what is expected.
