Duties

We are required to do physical work to beautify facilities directly related to our life in the camp. That includes mess halls, bathrooms and latrines, and also cleaning in and around the barracks and digging to build decks. We are all obligated to follow the orders. However, our understanding is that we have no obligation of physical work for the space outside of the camp beyond the barbed wire. We are supposed to be compensated if we voluntarily accept requests from the administration and offer our labor. It should be clearly understood as stated by the Consul General of Spain in Fort Sill. However, we are forced to render such physical labor almost every day here, and the elderly are obligated to work beyond their physical ability. As a result of negotiation made by our representatives, it was discovered that the authority has different understandings here. The administration ignores the existence of the barbed wire fence regarding our labor as they include the entire camp as the premises. I have to say that does not make any sense at all.  I heard their response was that they were going to check with the federal government and it would take several days to get back to us. (We were told that on June 18th) Some elderly people encountered physically demanding tasks while working in the facility. Several of them had to carry large lumbers to a vehicle and unload them at another soldier barrack. They had to perform this unfamiliar duty under scorching heat at 100 degrees. They were of course somehow compensated, but this should never be forced.
