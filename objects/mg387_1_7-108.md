378

I heard that some of the detainees have more than 10,000 dollars held by the government, but the majority have just a small amount brought with them and occasionally receive money sent by the family. Those families also face forced removal and do not have means to generate income, and they cannot afford waste their savings either. 

Meals have seen some improvement since Japanese personnel were assigned to be in charge of food, but there are still many aspects that we are not satisfied with. We sometimes even were provided with only one slice of bread. Shortage of milk is especially the biggest nutritional issue. Some angry members urge us to fast for two days or to throw away meals without eating them, and I am with them, but it is absolutely impossible to do for those who are complaining about the small portion. We heard that a correspondence inspector said some of us are complaining about the small potion while others are e xpressing satisfaction, and he asked which one is the true opinion of ours. We have never had such unpalatable meals. People might say we are spoiled with good food, but we lead such a life as we simply believe life is for us to enjoy. 

Patients

Those who need to see a doctor are to report in advance and go to the hospital facility outside the fence in a group with a guard at a certain time in the morning. Dr. Tanaka is the main doctor to see us, and he has 20 to 40 patients every day.
