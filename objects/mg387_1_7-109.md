379

Exchange to go back 

The first exchange boat for diplomats and others of both Japan and the U.S. departed from New York on June 18th. About 30 company employees and others from this camp were gradually sent to the port by guards. The first group of people who were ordered to return including Mr. Ichio Sato were added and left the camp at 4 in the afternoon of July 4th. They are scheduled to meet their families in New York. According to newspaper reports, the exchange boat (Swedish boat) delayed the departure and finally took off on the 18th. I am deeply disappointed that I did not get to be selected to be on this first boat. Such a departure order is unpredictable, and it comes at a moment's notice. Those who applied to go back to Japan should always be ready. 
(garden)

Mr. Onbarracko already planted seeds of vegetables, cosmos and asutas [4] in the empty space in front of our K-A # 1906 unit. Cosmos was brought from Camp Sill, and they had red and white flowers shortly after, offering a truly beautiful red-colored spot in this harsh surroundings. When I see those flowers in the early mornings when I get up, they conjure up warm feelings for my eyes in my tired heart that have been roughed up by an all male only environment. Tender emotions are tremendously sad and beautiful for us who are detained. It is also an unexpected love for nature felt by city people.
