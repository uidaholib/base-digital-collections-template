503

Below is a summary of what Consul-General of Spain and Japanese representative reported in a session they held at the relocation center [2], as well as response from the Relocation Director Myer [3] to the questions sent by the Welfare Department regarding the newly-constructed internee family center.

- The internee family center is located in Crystal City near the Mexico border in the southwest region of Texas State. The facility is situated among orange and olive trees on a hill and has capacity to hold approximately 2,000 people. Small families are to be transferred first as the number of all the internees and their family members exceed its maximum capacity. 

- Japanese Americans under age 18 will be accepted, but they are also allowed to move to a different location if they wish. School system is included in the plan, and high school graduates will be allowed to attend college if they wish.

- Once transferred to the camp, it is not allowed, U.S. citizens or not,  to leave even with a job offered outside of the camp. 

- Each family is to be provided with food for them to freely cook in the barracks. Personal properties owned by nisei will be under control of the administration and can be withdrawn by submitting a request. 

- It is not allowed to request to go back to Japan after being transferred to the camp, and the only option is to stay until the war is over. Release from the camp is not expected until security is confirmed.


[2] This is the term used by the author

[3] Director of the War Relocation Authority Dillon Myer
