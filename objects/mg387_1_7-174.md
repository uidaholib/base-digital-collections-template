444 

A monk from Hawaii who is a Russo-Japanese War veteran held a talk about his experience of being a war prisoner titled war prisoner story. According to him, it was recognized that he was captured by the enemy by cause beyond his control, and he was awarded an imperial national flag for his distinguished service. The monk told us that about 600 Japanese war prisoners were held in Russia back then, and each of them was treated with respect by the Japanese government after they came back. Second class driver Shibata among us was also a war prisoner and sent away with Major Mizoguchi when Wasomaru was attacked and sank. His story reminded me of Shibata's experience, but the monk's tale is not impressive and lacks detailed observations. I think to myself that my memory of Shibata's story is a more romantic [1] story and as secretly proud of myself. However, the monk's story wiped my worries away about Second Lieutenant Sakamaki. This young officer finished ocean navigation after he graduated from a military school and was sent out to serve immediately after being appointed. Moreover, he was engaged in the brave fight as a submarine officer when Japan conducted the first fierce attack. He also encountered misfortune on the way back to the homeland, and I would say no one is more qualified to be honored. Not to mention general Japanese citizens, but we internees also should pay our highest respect and deepest sympathy to him. However, Second Lieutenant Sakamaki Seems to shut his mouth and avoid encountering others. I am sorry that I have not got a chance to directly let him know how we feel about him.


[1] This is the term used by the author
