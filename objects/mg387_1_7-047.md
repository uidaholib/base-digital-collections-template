217

Raindrops were falling occasionally. Cheers from the Italians started again. As we were walking forward with our heavy feet, remaining fellow citizens in the facility were lined up on the wire fence, waving their hands and handkerchiefs, and shouting goodbye over and over again. I could clearly see some people crying. I saw my friends calling my names and getting choked up on the right side by side. Both Asaba and Nagamatsu in tears were bidding farewell to me. I was not sure if I was emotional or feeling numb. I was thinking of my situation where I am like a prisoner of war and being guarded and sent two thousand miles away leaving the Missoula camp behind under cloudy sky. I just raised my hand in response without a word, thinking this walk was not in my dream. However, I could not help finding energy in my bloodstream and feeling courage filling up my heart. 

Over 10 train cars were hooked together and waiting for us on the railroad going through a field. Our names were called again, and we were going on board as we called. It was great that the seats were arranged to have one seat occupied by one person as I predicted. Mr. Masaichi Samimura and I took a seat facing each other. The train departed at 7 p.m.

The barracks in Missoula where we had been enduring hardship were lined up far away in the late spring foggy evening. I could not see anyone and was wondering if a faint trail of smoke was coming up from the kitchen. I do not miss the place but do tremendously miss the remaining fellow citizens.
