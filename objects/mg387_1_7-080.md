Q: Would it be possible to obtain a copy of the Geneva Convention minutes regarding the treatment of prisoners of war?

A: We will provide it through  Major Eckel. 

Q: We would like to obtain written regulations of the prisoners of war exchange. 

A: The exchange agreement was negotiated entirely by telegrams, and we do not have any written regulations. 

Q: Would it be possible to correspond with the family if we leave them behind and go back?

A: It would be possible through the Red Cross. Representatives from the Red Cross are visiting the camp to meet with the internees. 

Q: Would it be possible for those who has permanent resident status to maintain the right to enter the U.S. again after he they go back to the homeland?

A: It depends on the result of the treaty.

Q: Who covers the traveling cost to New York for the families from Hawaii?

A: The U.S. government will cover the cost.

Q: Would it be possible to gather the family members of those who would wish to go back to a convenient location before departure?

A: We do not know.

Q: Is there an age limit for those who wish to go back?

A: Anyone at age 21 or older needs to obtain a special permission, but it is possible to go back for anyone else.

Q: Will the luggages be inspected at the departure?

A: There will be no inspection upon leaving the camp as long as there are no items  missing that are owned by the government. Inspection should be expected upon boarding the boat.
