501

Mr. Ikken Momii had a talk this afternoon about the relocation center [1] in central Utah where his family is incarcerated and the newly-constructed internees family center. He published a newspaper in San Francisco for one week several months before the war started. He wishes to return to Japan since he was detained and is one of the people who have been waiting for an opportunity to be on an exchange boat. He is from Fukuoka Prefecture. I listed some bullet points from his talk below. He was permitted to go to the center to hold a funeral for his wife. 

- Two guards were assigned to accompany Mr. Momii, and the captain strictly ordered him not to speak publicly in the center after he leaves. The guards were ordered to eliminate any danger around him and protect Mr. Momii. 

- He had plenty of time to watch a movie with the guard when they were waiting for the train to depart in Texas State.

- Both guards are college graduates, and one is 23 years old and the other is 25. They are both younger than his children, and they maintained sympathetic and respectful attitudes toward him, treating him as a gentleman. 

- He wanted to have a meal in Japantown in the city of Denver while they were waiting for the train. He was told that Americans are not strictly prohibited to shop in Japanese residential areas by the authorities when he went to a Japanese restaurant with the American guards. 


[1] This is the term used by the author
