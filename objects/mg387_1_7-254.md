524

What I find surprising is the acts and remarks made by Mr. Shigeo Okazaki. He used to be the head of the calm and quiet group and had a fairly subjective view and understanding of the war between Japan and the U.S. I considered being an internee sas honor we need to accept as a sacrifice as a Japanese citizen, and despise a thought of redress or longing for the family, maintaining his stable attitudes. However, it seems that he recently went through some major shifts in his mental state. When a soldier was walking around, calling out names in a loud voice and delivering mail the other day, he rushed from his bed to the window on the south end of the barracks a lot faster than usual, and intensely watched in the direction the voice was coming from looking worried. He looked so tense that I was worried about him, patted his shoulder and said, "Don't worry. It is not for you." He said, "I thought it was a release notice for me," and went back to his bed on the north end with a look I cannot describe, either feeling relieved or depressed. Mr. Kosaku Sasaki casually spread a false rumor saying, "Those who are to be released are lined up and waiting in front of the office," shortly after the above mentioned report was released. Mr. Okazaki went to the window to watch, looking worried, but said, "Ah, it is a lie," and sheepishly went back. He still seemed worried and ended up putting his wooden clogs on and went out. We cannot help feeling worried when we witness such behaviors.
