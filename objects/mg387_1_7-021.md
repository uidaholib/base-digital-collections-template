211

March 29 (Sunday)

We held two indoor ball games against each barrack today, one in the morning and the other one in the afternoon. The California team was holding up flags and drumming empty barrels, and it was quite a bustling sight like opening drumming for a grand sumo tournament or loud and comical street musicians hired for advertisement. 

Late spring air is all over the sky and ground in Missoula. A sash of leftover snow is still winding on the side of the mountain peaks far away, and such scenery in nature intensifies yearning for home. Despite my sorrow, I saw many of my fellow residents walking in the sunlight. 

On the radio this evening, it was reported that legal mediations are being made for Japanese living in Portland. Curfew law was issued by the military order this time to be enforced on enemy aliens and Japanese Americans. Mr. Minoru Yasui (a nisei lawyer), the eldest son of Mr. Masuo Yasui, deliberately broke the curfew law implemented by the military and turned himself in to present a test case and to challenge the constitutionality of the discriminatory treatment of citizens even though the target is Japanese descendants. 

Institution and human rights are the founding principal of the United States, and judgments of the U.S. Supreme Court will be closely watched.
