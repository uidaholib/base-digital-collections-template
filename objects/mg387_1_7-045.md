They finally decided to add inspectors, and several soldiers were assigned for the task. We hurried through the area for the Italians, went through the last gate and walked toward the front of the Recreation Hall. Four or five private soldiers were lined up in front of the hall entrance and patted us down on the pockets of our clothes and carry-on bags. As they are soldiers, they are not slow like INS officers. I took out a pipe cleaner and tweezer out of my pocket and handed them over. Knives are strictly restricted, and we were expected to hand them over to be kept by the administration. There were a bunch of INS officers and enlisted officers inside by the entrance after we went through it. We signed each of the two documents with the number of luggages sent in advance and the above-mentioned items left with them typed in, and also each of three or four documents with our photos and fingerprints glued on. They checked the front-view and side-view photos to match the person at the next spot, and that was the end of the procedure.  It was hours after we left to finally be able to sit and relax on a bench in the building. This marked we have been moved from the INS surveillance to the army surveillance. Army guards carrying a gun were leisurely watching us at each entrance and exit in the hall. I had a bit of a prisoner of war feelings. 

After everyone was inspected, our names were called again in alphabetical order, and we lined  up in the clearing outside of the hall asn we were called.
