April 6 (Monday)

Our new clerk Mr. Mihara came over to our barracks around 8:30 p.m. He started with "I have some bad news," and reported that orders to transfer a part of the detainees to another location have been issued and read the names of those who are scheduled to be moved. It was literally like a sudden and loud lightning strike.  The names are as follows:

Yasui, Fukuda, Ito, Ishibashi, Matsuura, Inoue, Miiyako, Sato, Tachioka [Tateoka], Harada, Nakamichi, Moringa, Ono, Sakimura, Hirayama, Shinome, Kondo, Haragami, Takeoka, Higashi [Azuma], Higashino [Azumano], Ikegami, Koyama (Kei), Tsuboi, Sakaino, Kobayashi, Tomihiro, Inuzuka, Okazaki, Okamoto, Toyama [1], Nishino, Tanaka and Kato. 

Therefore, all of us in the barracks except for Gen Koyama, Matsushima brothers, Kadota [Sumida], Nakajo and Yamamoto are to be transferred, and the destination was not announced. We were simply told that the travel distance would take three days and nights to cover to the south, and a telegram to the families is specified to uniformly be "Being moved don't write until hear from me". 

Shock and astonishment of everyone is hard to describe, and I am having a difficult time finding an adjective for it. Splashing with cold water is understatement to describe the shock I am experiencing. We were not able to utter a word for a while. However, as time went by, our desperate fury ended up exploding. Could it be North Dakota or New Mexico? We were told south, so it is probably New Mexico.
