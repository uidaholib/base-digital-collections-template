July 16 (Thursday)

Milk was offered to all of us for the first time today. This is the first milk served since we moved to this camp. What a pity. It is true that it is relatively a minority, out of many Japanese, who were including  milk in our daily diet before incarceration. However, milk is essential as a nutritious supplement for the unbalanced diet currently offered, and we guzzle it down regardless if we like it or not. 

The Alexandria Town Talk Daily (evening edition) issued on the 15th carries a report of a visit to our camp. Tens of copies were brought into the canteen stores with the shipping slip displayed, and they are being sold for five cents a copy. Based on what I heard from the officer in charge, they are offering to arrange shipment of copies to our family. The article was written by a reporter who toured in the camp guided by Colonel Dunn, and he wrote about Second Lieutenant Sakamaki. He started his piece by stating that the second lieutenant is not answering any questions whatsoever and he rapidly writes in English in clear and grammatically correct fashion.
